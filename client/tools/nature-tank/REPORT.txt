FIXES
- Two persistent closed tread loops, 32 shoes per side (64 total), animated
  from left/right travel and following the eight suspension stations.
- A forward-facing driver seat and actual ctx.driver / ctx.input controls.
- Ground height comes from ray-origin Y minus hit distance. The returned
  voxel position is its integer origin, one metre below its upper face.
- Suspension probes follow wheel centers when the hull pitches. Horizontal
  probes locate ledge faces, then vertical probes find their true top. Tire
  clearance uses the circular contact envelope and a small swept margin.
- A bounded retry defers only an untouched frame whose WASM preparation
  stalled before any component tick or host operation. At most two such
  deferrals are allowed; guest execution, aggregate time, memory and fuel
  failures still stop immediately. Existing 50 ms / 250 ms budgets remain.

MODEL
8,495 voxels, 10 components, 64 decoration shoes, one seat, one 20 Hz script.
Dynamic 3,500 kg hull with gravity and collision. Eight independently sprung
kinematic wheel visuals; tire radius 0.875 m and tread outer radius 1.035 m.
Spring 16,000 N/m; damping 2,300 Ns/m; travel limited to 0.12..1.25 m.
The hull moves through forces and torques, not scripted pose/velocity changes.
Contact is a raycast suspension approximation, not eight rigid tire colliders
or a physically simulated linked chain. All queries fit the 64-ray tick cap.

ACTUAL BROWSER VALIDATION
The existing 344-block proving ground has a flat top at Y=25 and a vertical
one-metre step at Y=26, Z=634..637. No ramp.
V successfully mounted and dismounted the seat. Native held-key tests verified
W/S, A/D and Space. Final forward braking reduced planar speed below 0.01 m/s.
A/D changed yaw in opposite directions; unmounted W is ignored in the script.

A continuous 92.50-second run recorded 926 samples and six complete
crossings (three each way), including whole-vehicle clearance on each side.
Every sample used the final controller, stayed running, and had no script error.
Across 7,408 wheel samples, minimum nominal tire-bottom clearance above the
surface directly beneath its center was 0.0732 m. No sampled nominal tire envelope
was below that surface. This is sampled evidence, not continuous collision proof.
Suspension lengths ranged 0.12..1.25 m, with up to 0.992 m difference
between wheels at one instant. The final screenshot shows the tank straddling
the step with the rear road wheels raised and the lower track on the floor.

The tank adds 149 draw calls in the paired fixed view. Visible versus hidden
costs about 1.4 ms of median main-thread frame work. The moving seated view is
a separate workload and must not be subtracted as a controlled frame-time delta.
A preliminary 80-shoe version measured about 74 FPS while driving; 64 shoes
measured about 92 FPS here. These are sequential observations, not a universal
speedup guarantee. Animation still costs more than idling.
CPU timings exclude GPU completion, other browser tasks and worker CPU. RAF
cadence is not calibrated display-presented FPS. Results depend on resolution,
camera, terrain, concurrent entities and host load.

TREAD JITTER FOLLOW-UP - 2026-10-13
Decoration transforms previously snapped to each 20 Hz script result while the
hull and wheels interpolated between fixed updates. Decorations now interpolate
position, shortest-path quaternion rotation and scale using the same history;
after rendering, solved transforms are restored and checkpoints remain unchanged.
Tread rotation now blends adjacent path tangents instead of snapping 22.5 degrees
at each wheel-arc vertex. A normalized cycle preserves phase as suspension travel
changes the perimeter. The 64-shoe, 64-ray and 256-command limits are unchanged.
A bounded preparation retry now also covers pauses before WASM instantiation,
when allocating/clearing memory. Guest startup/execution and resource failures
remain non-retryable; retries remain limited to two untouched frames.

The original local tank was updated in place. A 36-second browser trial crossed
the one-metre step forward and backward without any script error. Comparing the
same updated trajectory, rendering interpolation reduced local shoe displacement
P95 from 0.0946 m to 0.0523 m per sampled render frame. Peak angular change fell
from 10.40 to 5.70 degrees. This measures interpolation, not the full old/new
controller or FPS; the summary records sampling exclusions and exact values.
The original position/orientation and stopped state were restored after testing.
