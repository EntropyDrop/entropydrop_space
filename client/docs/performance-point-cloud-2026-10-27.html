Opaque point-cloud terrain

Three real implementations: native one-pixel points, opaque square surfels and a compute rasterizer with deterministic depth/ID reduction. Only eligible terrain beyond 768 m changes. Original PBR and a matching simple-light rectangle renderer are both measured. See the full report.

2560 × 1440, Medium, shadows off, 4× MSAA for the scene. Native points use hardware sample coverage; compute points resolve one whole pixel and do not reproduce that coverage. GPU span includes every compute pass and the depth-writing composite. Each terrain has 40 interleaved blocks across turning and moving while looking across the ring.

Result: keep packed terrain geometry. Surfels preserve coverage much better than single-pixel points, but have no convincing speed advantage over the matched rectangle control. Copper movement improves versus original PBR with both representations. Point storage grows, and compute adds GPU work. Near-120 FPS rows are refresh-limited.
